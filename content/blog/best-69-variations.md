## Safer Oral: Dental Dams and Condoms

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

Oral sex can pass STIs, including herpes, gonorrhea, syphilis, and HPV. You can still have plenty of fun. Just use the right barrier.

**For a vulva or anus: dental dams.** A [dental dam](/define/dental-dam/) is a thin square of latex or polyurethane. Lay it flat over the vulva or anus and lick through it. A little water-based lube on the receiver's side makes it feel better. Keep the same side facing the body, and use a new one each time [[AFF: dental dams]]. In a pinch, you can make one from a condom: cut off the tip and the ring, slice it down one side, and open it flat.

**For a penis: condoms.** A condom on the penis protects both of you during oral. Flavored condoms are made for this, and they taste better than regular ones.

**Other smart habits:**
- Skip oral if either of you has a cold sore or any sore, bump, or rash on the mouth or genitals.
- Get tested regularly, and talk about results with new partners.
- Ask your doctor about the HPV vaccine.
- See a doctor if you notice sores, unusual discharge, a sore throat that won't go away, or pain after sex.

## FAQ

### How do you do 69 comfortably?

Try the side-by-side version, where you both lie on your sides and no one holds anyone's weight. If you prefer the classic version, put firm pillows under the bottom partner's head so they don't have to crane their neck. The top partner can rest on their forearms. ### Why is it called 69?

The name comes from the shape of the numbers 6 and 9. Put them side by side and they look like two bodies lying head to toe, each one curled toward the other. The term is also known by its French name, soixante-neuf, which simply means sixty-nine.
