### Talk first (yes, out loud)

Talking first feels awkward. Do it anyway. It's the easiest way to feel safe.

[Consent](/define/consent/) means everyone wants to be there, and it's an ongoing yes. You can say yes to kissing and no to sex. You can say yes to sex and change your mind halfway through. A good partner stops right away, with no sulking.

Easy things to say ahead of time:

- "I'm nervous, so can we go really slow?"
- "If I say stop, I need you to stop right away."
- "Can we use a condom?"

The right person will be relieved you brought it up.

### Condoms and birth control

A [condom](/define/condom/) protects against pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Use one from the start, even if you're also on birth control.

- **Practice first.** He can practice putting one on alone. Fumbling in the moment is less stressful when you've done it before.
- **Check the fit.** Condoms come in sizes. Too tight is uncomfortable and more likely to break. Too loose can slip off. A variety pack helps.
- **Pinch the tip** as you roll it on, and check the date on the wrapper.

For extra pregnancy protection, talk to a doctor or clinic about birth control before your first time. If a condom breaks or slips, emergency contraception is an option, and a pharmacist can tell you what's available. If either of you has had partners before, getting tested for STIs is a kind, grown-up move.

### Lube is your best friend

Even if you usually get wet easily, nerves can dry things up. [Lube](/define/lube/) is one of the best ways to avoid pain the first time.

Pick a **water-based lube**. It's safe with every condom and toy. Skip coconut oil, lotion, or baby oil with latex condoms, because oil weakens latex and can make a condom break. Use more than you think you need, on the condom and on your vulva, and keep the bottle within reach.

### Foreplay is the main event

[Foreplay](/define/foreplay/) is everything before penetration: kissing, touching, oral, fingers, a vibrator on your clit. For a first time, it's the main event.

When you're turned on, your body gets ready. You get wetter, and your vagina relaxes and opens up a bit. Rushing past that is one of the most common reasons first-time sex hurts. Lots of women also need clit stimulation to orgasm, so make sure your clit gets plenty of attention, before and during.

### Go slow, then slower

There's no rush and no finish line. A gentle way to build up:

1. Kissing and touching.
2. Fingers on your clit and vulva.
3. One finger inside, slowly. Then two, if that feels good.
4. The tip of the penis, with lube, and a pause.
5. A little more, only when you say so.

You can stop at any step and try again another night. Plenty of couples need a few tries before penetration feels easy.

## Does the first time hurt? The truth about the hymen

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

Honest answer: it might feel a little uncomfortable, but it shouldn't really hurt. A stretching or pressure feeling is common. Sharp pain is a sign to stop and change something.

### The hymen doesn't "pop"

You've probably heard that the [hymen](/define/hymen/) "breaks" the first time. That's a myth.

The hymen is a thin, stretchy rim of tissue around the vaginal opening. It usually already has an opening (that's how period blood gets out). Hymens come in lots of shapes, and many stretch over time from tampons, sports, or masturbation.

During sex, the hymen might stretch, or tear a tiny bit and cause light spotting, or nothing noticeable happens. Lots of people don't bleed at all. And nobody can tell whether you've had sex by looking at your hymen.

### What actually causes first-time pain

- **Not enough arousal.** If your body isn't ready, it doesn't relax or get wet.
- **Not enough lube.** Friction is the enemy.
- **Tense muscles.** Nerves make your pelvic floor clench.
- **Going too fast or too deep.** Depth is something to build up to.
- **An awkward angle.** A small shift or a pillow often fixes it.

If penetration hurts a lot every time, even with foreplay and lube, see a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist. Sometimes the muscles around the vagina tense up on their own (this is called vaginismus), and it can be treated.

## What to do if it hurts

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

Stretchy or full feelings are common. Sharp or stinging pain is your body asking you to change something. Try these, in order:

1. **Stop moving.** Ask your partner to stay still or pull out.
2. **Add more lube.** This alone fixes a lot.
3. **Go back to foreplay.** Let your body catch up.
4. **Breathe out slowly.** Long exhales help your muscles let go.
5. **Change position.** Try cowgirl or lazy cowgirl so you control the depth.
6. **Go shallower.** Just the tip, moving slowly, still counts.
7. **Call it a night.** You can try again another day, and that's often when it clicks.

If pain keeps happening, lasts after sex, or comes with heavy bleeding, see a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist.

## Aftercare: what to do after your first time

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

![Hero: two clothed figures cuddling on a bed, smiling, soft and sweet](/images/first-time-hero.webp)

[Aftercare](/define/aftercare/) means taking care of each other once sex is over. It's extra nice after a first time.

**Cuddle and check in.** Stay close, have a snack, and ask, "How are you feeling?" Happy, emotional, giggly, a little weird: every one of those is normal.

**Pee after sex.** It helps flush out bacteria and helps prevent urinary tract infections (UTIs). Keep the habit for life.

**Clean up gently.** Warm water is all your vulva needs. Skip douches and scented soaps. Toss the condom in the trash, not the toilet.

**Expect a little tenderness.** Feeling a bit sore the next day can happen, and light spotting is common. Heavy bleeding, pain that doesn't fade, or unusual discharge, itching, or burning means it's time to see a doctor.

**Talk about it later.** A day or two after, chat about what you liked. "I loved when you kissed my neck" is a great opener. First times are rarely anyone's best sex ever, and talking is how the next time gets better.

## FAQ

### What is the best position to lose your virginity?

Cowgirl is a great pick for most first-timers, because the person being penetrated is on top and controls the speed, depth, and angle. Nothing happens unless she lowers herself. If you'd rather be on your back, missionary with a pillow under your hips (the pillow driver) is comfortable and lets you kiss and talk. Spooning and the hug are good if you want something slow and shallow.

### Does the first time hurt?

It might feel a bit uncomfortable or stretchy, but it shouldn't be painful. Most first-time pain comes from not enough arousal, not enough lube, tense muscles, or going too fast. Lots of foreplay, plenty of water-based lube, and a position where you control the depth help a lot. If penetration hurts every time, even with lube and foreplay, see a doctor or a pelvic floor physical therapist.

### How long does first-time sex last?

There's no right length. Penetration might last a few minutes, or the whole thing (foreplay included) might take an hour. It's often short the first time, especially if your partner is excited or nervous, and that's normal. Try not to watch the clock. Sex doesn't have to end when he finishes, either, because fingers, oral, and toys still count.

### Should I use lube for my first time?

Yes. Lube is one of the best ways to make your first time comfortable, even if you get wet easily, because nerves can dry things up. Choose a water-based lube, which is safe with every condom and toy. Avoid coconut oil or lotion with latex condoms, since oil weakens latex. Add more any time things start to feel draggy.

### Do you bleed the first time?

Some people do and many don't. The hymen is a stretchy rim of tissue that usually already has an opening, and it doesn't "pop." It might stretch, tear a tiny bit and cause light spotting, or do nothing noticeable. Not bleeding says nothing about your body or your past. If you have heavy bleeding, or bleeding with a lot of pain, contact a doctor.

### What if he finishes too fast the first time?

That's really common, especially when someone is nervous or excited, so try not to make it a big deal. Laugh, cuddle, and keep going with fingers, oral, or a vibrator so you get your turn too.
