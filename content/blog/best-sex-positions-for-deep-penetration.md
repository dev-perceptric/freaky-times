## Why some positions go deeper

Depth isn't only about size. The same partner can feel shallow in one position and very deep in the next. Three things make the difference.

**Hips tilted.** When her pelvis tilts up, the vaginal canal lines up more directly with her partner. That lets them go further in. A pillow or wedge under her hips does this, and so does lifting her knees toward her chest.

**Legs up.** Raising her legs, onto her partner's shoulders or toward her chest, tilts the pelvis even more. It also moves her thighs and bottom out of the way, so there's less "buffer" between bodies. That's why [legs-up missionary](/define/legs-up-missionary/) tops so many deep lists.

**Gravity.** When she's on top, her own body weight pulls her down. She can sink all the way down, or hold herself up to keep it shallow. From behind, her partner can push deeper with every stroke.

## Deep vs. too deep

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

Deep can feel amazing. Too deep usually feels like a sharp jab or a dull ache inside. Here's what's going on, and how to stay on the good side.

### The cervix

The [cervix](/define/cervix/) is the lower end of the uterus. It sits at the top of the vagina. During deep penetration, the tip of a penis or toy can bump it.

Some women like a little pressure there. Many don't. A hard bump can feel sharp or crampy, and the ache can last after sex. Usually it's just uncomfortable. Treat it as a sign to change the angle or go slower.

Your cervix can also sit higher or lower at different points in your cycle. A position that feels great one week might bump the next week. That's normal.

### Arousal changes everything

When you're turned on, your body changes. The vagina gets longer, and the upper part expands. That gives more room for depth.

If you go deep before you're fully aroused, there's less space, and you're more likely to feel bumping or pain. So warm up first. Kissing, touching, oral, and toys all count. Wait until you really want it before going deep.

### The depth dial

Here's a simple way to control depth together. Think of depth as a dial from 1 to 5.

- **1:** Just the tip.
- **2:** Shallow. Halfway or less.
- **3:** Comfortable middle.
- **4:** Deep.
- **5:** All the way in.

She calls out the number. "Stay at a 2." "Take me to a 4." "Back to a 3." It's quick, it's clear, and it takes the guessing out of it. Her partner never has to wonder, and she never has to explain mid-moment.

Start every session at a 2 or 3. Only go up when she asks.

## When pain is a sign to see a doctor

[MEDICAL REVIEW]

Some bumping now and then is normal. Pain that keeps happening is worth checking out, and you don't have to just put up with it.

Pain during sex has a name: [dyspareunia](/define/dyspareunia/). Deep pain during sex can come from endometriosis and other conditions. [CITE: common causes of deep pain during sex] Pain can also come from tight pelvic floor muscles, infections, or dryness.

See a doctor if:
- Deep sex hurts most times you have it.
- Pain lasts for hours or days after sex.
- You bleed after sex when you're not on your period.
- You also have painful periods, pelvic pain, or pain when you pee or poop.
- The pain is sharp, sudden, or new.

A doctor can check for causes and help you find relief. If your muscles are tight or tense, a pelvic floor physical therapist can help too.
